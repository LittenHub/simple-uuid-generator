What is UUID/GUID?

• UUID (Universally Unique Identifier) is a 128-bit number used to uniquely identify information or objects in computer systems. Because they are generated locally by applications using massive random numbers or timestamps, you never need a central authority to guarantee that your ID is entirely unique.

• GUID stands for Globally Unique Identifier. It is a 128-bit reference number used in software and databases to uniquely identify information without needing a central authority to manage or assign the ID..

How it Works?

• Format: It is represented as a 36-character alphanumeric string (32 hex characters separated by hyphens in an 8-4-4-4-12 pattern).

• The Odds of Duplication: A 128-bit number has roughly 3.4 x 10³⁸ possible combinations. The mathematical probability of two independent systems generating the exact same UUID is practically zero.

• No Central Coordination: You and another developer across the globe could independently generate UUIDs at the exact same time without communicating, and your IDs will still be distinct.

Why developer use them?

• Distributed Systems: Perfect for applications operating across multiple servers where assigning traditional sequential IDs (like 1, 2, 3) requires a central server to coordinate traffic.

• Security & Privacy: Sequential IDs in URLs (e.g., ://website.com) allow malicious actors to guess your data volume or other users' IDs. UUIDs are completely unpredictable.

• Merging Databases: If you merge two completely different databases into one, you don't have to worry about clashing or overlapping primary keys.
